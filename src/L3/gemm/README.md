# gemm — general matrix multiplication

$$C \leftarrow \alpha\,\mathrm{op}(A)\,\mathrm{op}(B) + \beta C,\qquad
\mathrm{op}(X) \in \{X,\ X^{T},\ X^{H}\}$$

$A$ is $m \times k$ after `op`, $B$ is $k \times n$, $C$ is $m \times n$;
each has its own leading dimension. Four routines, one file each:
`dgemm` (f64), `sgemm` (f32), `zgemm` (c64), `cgemm` (c32). The
signature follows netlib:

```text
dgemm(transa, transb, m, n, k, alpha, a, lda, b, ldb, beta, c, ldc, work)
```

`work` is a scratch slice of at least `dgemm_work(transa, transb, m,
n, k)` elements; the query is proportional to the problem (the panels
below), zero when a dimension is zero. A slice shorter than the query
is an argument error, never a silent slower path.

## What the result is, exactly

Every element of $C$ is computed as one sequence of roundings, the
same in every shape this family ships and on every machine:
$\beta c_{ij}$ first, then the products added in ascending $l$,

$$c_{ij} \leftarrow \mathrm{fl}\big(\cdots \mathrm{fl}\big(\mathrm{fl}(\beta c_{ij}) + \mathrm{fl}(\mathrm{fl}(\alpha b_{lj})\, a_{il})\big) \cdots\big),\quad l = 0, 1, \ldots, k-1,$$

with $\alpha$ applied to $B$'s element before the product. That order
is the plain column-axpy definition (`*gemm_colaxpy`, a matrix–vector
product per column of $C$), and every tuned shape reproduces it bit
for bit: the tests cross-check all of them on shapes that cross every
block boundary (`tests/L3/dgemm.rs` and the three twins). The
reference's special cases hold: $\alpha = 0$ or $k = 0$ scales $C$
without reading $A$ or $B$ ($\beta = 1$ leaves it untouched, $\beta = 0$
writes zeros without reading $C$). A NaN's sign and payload are
outside the promise; everything finite is inside it.

## How it is computed

**f64 and f32: the packed-panel shape with a broadcast micro-kernel.**
For each block of $K_C = 256$ values of $l$, $B$'s block is packed
into a contiguous panel in quads of columns with $\alpha$ already
multiplied in, and $A$'s block is packed in panels of $M_R$ rows
($M_R = 4$ in f64, 8 in f32) over at most $M_C = 128$ rows at a time.
The micro-kernel then computes one $M_R \times 4$ tile of $C$ over
the whole block: per step of $l$ it loads $A$'s $M_R$ values as two
vectors, loads $B$'s four values as one or two vectors, broadcasts each
of the four across the lanes with a shuffle, and does eight multiplies
and eight adds into eight accumulators that live in registers for the
entire block. Nothing scalar is left in the loop. This is the shape of
OpenBLAS's own WebAssembly kernel and of BLIS's generic kernel; the
block sizes were swept (120–256 / 64–256) and moved nothing.

The row tail ($m \bmod M_R$ rows) and the column tail ($n \bmod 4$
columns) take the same terms in the same order through the plain
four-column stream, so they are bit-identical too.

**c64 and c32: a fused stream small, a complex tile large.** Below a
size threshold on $A$ (1 MB in c64, 8 MB in c32) the complex gemms run
four columns of $C$ at a time through a fused column-axpy stream
(`zaxpy4` / `caxpy4` in `src/kernels.rs`); above it they pack as the
real types do and run a complex register tile ($2 \times 4$ complexes
in c64, $4 \times 4$ in c32). Complex arithmetic does four real
multiplies per element, so these kernels were already compute-bound
and ahead of OpenBLAS before the K2 campaign; they were not changed by
it. The thresholds are the verdicts of two-draw runner races recorded
with the shapes.

**Transposed operands are packed from a transposed read.** $\mathrm{op}(A)$'s
row is a stored column of $A$, so the packer reads it directly; the
three tails that read operands unpacked are gathered into the
workspace. This is decision D3's winner over copying the whole operand
first (`*gemm_copy_op`, kept and exported); c32 routes the copy for a
transposed $B$ alone, where the pack lost every row. Conjugation is an
exact sign flip, so every flag form equals the no-transpose call on an
explicit transpose, bit for bit, and that equality is a test.

**Exported shapes.** Each file exports its plain reference and the
losers of its races beside the routed kernel, so a race can be re-run
and a routing reversed by a dispatch change: `*gemm_colaxpy` (the
definition), `*gemm_col4` (the fused four-column stream), and in f64
and f32 also `*gemm_tiled` (the unpacked register tile that served
small sizes until the K2 kernel beat it) and `*gemm_packed` (the packed
shape with the pre-K2 kernel). The kernels take `(alpha, m, n, k, a,
lda, b, ldb, beta, c, ldc[, work])`, no flags, and assert rather than
return errors.

## Performance

**The K2 race** (`bench/k2-ab.mjs`): the broadcast kernel against the
pre-K2 packed shape, both called directly with the workspace outside
the timed call, folds asserted identical. Figures are `old ÷ new` wall
time, the range across runner runs 40226735030 and 40226834978:

| shape | f64 | f32 |
|---|---|---|
| 256³ | 1.87–1.90 | 1.77–1.91 |
| 512³ | 1.86–1.89 | 1.78–1.91 |
| 1024³ | 1.82–1.86 | 1.79–1.91 |
| 128×2048×2048 | 1.80–1.83 | 1.73–1.86 |

Against the unpacked register tile below its old threshold the new
kernel won 1.22× at 128³ rising to 2.27× at 512³ in f64 and 1.22–1.55×
in f32 (dev container, one draw; the margins are far outside the
runner's noise), so the tile's zone closed and the packed kernel
routes at every size.

**Against OpenBLAS and BLIS in the same engine** (the Level 3 README
has the table and the method): dgemm ties OpenBLAS-wasm (1.00–1.05× of
its time at 512³ and 1024³), sgemm trails it by 11–17%, zgemm leads it
by 5–6% and cgemm by 35–41%; all four lead BLIS's generic build, dgemm
by about 2×, cgemm by 3×.

40212217524): `pack ÷ copy` wall time, every shape above the type's
packed threshold, β = 0, T standing for C on the complex types:

f64 and c64 route the pack for any transposed operand (never a loss);
f32 routes it as a tie on squares (draw 1 had 0.90–0.96 on five rows,
draw 2 ≥ 1.01 on every row, the two draws on runners of different
speed) and a win of 1.1–1.7× where $A$ is transposed and $n$ is small;
c32 routes the pack only when $A$ is transposed and the copy for a
$B$-only transpose, which lost every row (0.90–0.99). These rows
predate the K2 kernel; the pack's advantage is in avoiding the copy,
which the kernel change does not touch, but the race has not been
re-run since.

**Market comparison, not the metric:** against faer's blocked gemm,
raced two draws each in the predecessor repository, this family led
in every type (dgemm 1.25–1.8×, zgemm 1.5–1.7× at n ≥ 256, cgemm
3.1–3.7×). Those numbers predate the packed shapes and are kept for
the record only.

**What is left.** sgemm is the one real row behind OpenBLAS-wasm
(11–17%) with the same $8 \times 4$ geometry as its kernel. The
suspects, in order: the broadcast is a vector load plus four shuffles
where wasm has single-instruction load-and-splat ops; the f32 kernel
sits near V8's sixteen-register limit (eight accumulators, two of $A$,
one of $B$, four broadcasts) and may spill. The emitted wasm was read
for f64, not f32. Recorded in `STATUS.md` under "Levers".

## Tests

`tests/L3/dgemm.rs`, `sgemm.rs`, `zgemm.rs`, `cgemm.rs`: the bit-for-bit
replay of the per-element sequence above against a scalar
reimplementation, every exported shape cross-checked bit-identical on
sizes crossing the $K_C$, $M_C$ and $M_R$ boundaries, every flag form
equal to the no-transpose call on an explicit transpose, the workspace
NaN-filled so a read of unpacked scratch would show; `semantics.rs`
for the $\alpha = 0$, $k = 0$ and $\beta$ rules; `errors.rs` for every
argument check. The native ↔ wasm probe patterns are in
`tests/README.md`.
