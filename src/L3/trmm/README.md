# trmm — triangular matrix multiplication, in place

$$B \leftarrow \alpha\,\mathrm{op}(A)\,B \quad(\text{left}),\qquad
B \leftarrow \alpha\,B\,\mathrm{op}(A) \quad(\text{right}),$$

$A$ triangular (upper or lower, unit or non-unit diagonal), $m \times m$
on the left and $n \times n$ on the right; $B$ is $m \times n$ and is
overwritten. Four routines: `dtrmm`, `strmm`, `ztrmm`, `ctrmm`.

```text
dtrmm(side, uplo, transa, diag, m, n, alpha, a, lda, b, ldb, work)
```

`work` is `dtrmm_work(side, transa, m, n)` elements: the packed panels
for the real no-transpose forms, the copy plus the panels where a
transposed form routes the copy, zero where it routes a no-copy
kernel.

## What the result is, exactly

Each element of $B$ takes its terms in the order the original column
sweep used, and every shape reproduces that order bit for bit. Two
documented reorders exist and are mirrored by the tests' scalar
replay: on the right side with an upper $A$, the terms from inside a
group of four columns precede those from outside it; the other three
side/triangle combinations are the plain sweep's order. $\alpha = 0$
sets $B$ to zero without reading $A$ or $B$, as the reference does.
Reference BLAS skips a column whose multiplier is exactly zero; this
crate does not (a NaN or infinity in that column propagates), a
recorded deviation (`DESIGN.md`, constraint 6; decision D12).

## How it is computed

**f64 and f32: both sides on the packing layer** (`*trmm_left_packed`,
`*trmm_right_packed`, the K1 campaign). The off-diagonal terms go
through gemm's broadcast micro-kernel: on the left, $B$ is packed per
$k$-block as the right panel and the triangle's rows as the left
panel; on the right, $B$'s rows are packed once as the left panels and
$\alpha A$'s columns are the right panel. The diagonal blocks run
through a small lane routine (left) or the sweep's own in-group column
code (right), in place, and the row and column tails take the plain
sweep. Every term lands in the order the sweep used, so the result is
the sweep's bits.

**c64 and c32: the lockstep column sweeps.** Left: four columns of $B$
walk the `trmv` column sweep in lockstep, so each column of $A$
streams once per four. Right: the in-group terms in the original
elimination order, then the out-of-group columns fanned out one
stream each. These are the shapes the real types shipped before K1
(`*trmm_left_sweep`, `*trmm_right_sweep`, still exported in f64 and
f32 as the tests' references).

**Transposed forms.** Decision D3's race: on the right, every type
reads $\mathrm{op}(A)$ through a transposed accessor (the right-side
kernels only ever read $A$ as scalars), no copy; on the left, f64 and
f32 run column-reduction kernels that walk $\mathrm{op}(A)$'s rows as
stored columns (`*trmm_stream`), while c64 and c32 copy $A$ transposed
into the workspace and run the no-transpose shape (`*trmm_copy`). The
copy candidate now runs the packed kernel on its copy, so the race
below predates the kernel on that side; re-running it is the closing
question of D3 in `STATUS.md`.

The stream is routed on the right in every type and on the left in
f64 and f32 (the f32 ascending-walk case, trmm upper, sits at 0.94–1.0
on the squares and wins the skinny shapes); c64 and c32 route the copy
on the left, where the scalar complex reduction kernel loses every
256² and 1024×128 row.

**What is left.** The 15–23% behind OpenBLAS-wasm is in the parts that
are not the tile: the diagonal-block routine and the per-$k$-block
packing of $B$. That split is an estimate from the scoreboard, not a
profile; profiling per wasm function is the first step recorded in
`STATUS.md` under "Levers". A lane version of the complex left
transposed kernel is the other recorded lever.

## Tests

`tests/L3/dtrmm.rs`, `strmm.rs`, `ztrmm.rs`, `ctrmm.rs`: the bit-for-bit
replay of both sides (the right-upper reorder mirrored), bounds and
residuals; the packed shapes bit-identical to the exported sweeps for
m or n in 1..13 and 16, 17, 31, 64, 65, 130, 257, 260, 300, both
triangles, unit and non-unit, two $\alpha$, $A$'s unstored triangle
NaN-poisoned (two NaNs count as equal, the promise's stated limit);
every `transa` form, both candidates and the routed wrapper, equal to
the no-transpose call on an explicit transpose; `semantics.rs` for
$\alpha = 0$; `errors.rs` for the argument checks.
