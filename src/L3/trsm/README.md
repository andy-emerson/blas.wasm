# trsm — triangular solve with many right-hand sides, in place

$$B \leftarrow \alpha\,\mathrm{op}(A)^{-1} B \quad(\text{left}),\qquad
B \leftarrow \alpha\,B\,\mathrm{op}(A)^{-1} \quad(\text{right}),$$

$A$ triangular (upper or lower, unit or non-unit diagonal), $m \times m$
on the left and $n \times n$ on the right; $B$ is $m \times n$ and is
overwritten with the solution. Four routines: `dtrsm`, `strsm`,
`ztrsm`, `ctrsm`. No singularity check, as in the reference.

```text
dtrsm(side, uplo, transa, diag, m, n, alpha, a, lda, b, ldb, work)
```

`work` is `dtrsm_work(side, transa, m, n)` elements: the packed panels
for the real no-transpose forms, the copy plus the panels where a
transposed form routes the copy, zero where it routes a no-copy
kernel.

## What the result is, exactly

Each element of $B$ takes its terms in the order the original column
sweep used: $\alpha$ first, then the subtractions in elimination
order, then the diagonal. On the left the diagonal step is a division,
$b_{ij} \leftarrow b_{ij} / a_{ii}$, as the reference writes it; on
the right it is a multiplication by the reciprocal $1 / a_{jj}$,
again as the reference writes it (complex division by Smith's
algorithm, the complex type's guarded `/`). One documented reorder,
mirrored by the tests' replay: on the right with a lower $A$, the
terms from already-solved columns outside the group of four precede
the in-group ones. $\alpha = 0$ sets $B$ to zero without reading $A$
or $B$. The reference's zero-multiplier skip is not mirrored
(decision D12).

## How it is computed

**f64 and f32: both sides on the packing layer** (`*trsm_left_packed`,
`*trsm_right_packed`, the K1 campaign). Left: $B$ is scaled by
$\alpha$, then for each $k$-block and row block in elimination order
the already-solved rows enter through gemm's broadcast micro-kernel
from a panel that is appended as each block is solved (with the solved
rows negated, so the tile's adds are the sweep's subtractions), and
the diagonal block is solved on lanes with the solved rows kept in
registers. Right: the groups of four columns are solved in the sweep's
order, each scaled by $\alpha$, the solved columns' terms taken
through the tile from left panels appended as each group is solved,
then the sweep's own in-group elimination and reciprocal.

**c64 and c32: the lockstep column sweeps.** Left: $\alpha$-scale, then
four columns of $B$ walk the `trsv` elimination in lockstep. Right:
the already-solved out-of-group columns fanned in one stream each,
then the in-group elimination in the original order. These are the
shapes the real types shipped before K1 (`*trsm_left_sweep`,
`*trsm_right_sweep`, exported in f64 and f32 as the tests'
references).

**Transposed forms.** As for `trmm`: a transposed accessor on the right
in every type (no copy); column-reduction kernels on the left in f64
and f32 (`*trsm_stream`); the transposed copy into the workspace on the
left in c64 and c32 (`*trsm_copy`). The copy now runs the packed kernel
on its copy; the race below predates that, which is D3's closing
question in `STATUS.md`.

**What is left.** The 15–22% behind OpenBLAS-wasm on the left side is
in the diagonal solve and the per-block packing, by estimate, not
profile; profiling comes first (`STATUS.md`, "Levers"). BLIS's fused
triangular micro-kernel packs the diagonal's reciprocals and
multiplies inside the tile; on the left that would replace the
reference's division with a multiplication and change the bits, so it
is a decision, not a lever.

## Tests

`tests/L3/dtrsm.rs`, `strsm.rs`, `ztrsm.rs`, `ctrsm.rs`: the
bit-for-bit replay of both sides (the right-lower reorder mirrored),
bounds and residuals against the product; the packed shapes
bit-identical to the exported sweeps on the same shape list as `trmm`
(two NaNs count as equal); every `transa` form, both candidates and
the routed wrapper, equal to the no-transpose call on an explicit
transpose; `semantics.rs` for $\alpha = 0$; `errors.rs` for the
argument checks.
