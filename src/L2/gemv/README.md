# gemv — general matrix–vector multiplication

$$y \leftarrow \alpha\,\mathrm{op}(A)\,x + \beta y,\qquad
\mathrm{op}(A) \in \{A,\ A^{T},\ A^{H}\}$$

$A$ is $m \times n$; $x$ has $n$ elements and $y$ has $m$ before the
transpose, the other way after it. Four routines: `dgemv`, `sgemv`,
`zgemv`, `cgemv`; the complex ones take all three forms of `op`
(`ConjTrans` is the form complex algorithms consume), the real ones
treat `ConjTrans` as `Trans`.

```text
dgemv(trans, m, n, alpha, a, lda, x, beta, y)
```

## What the result is, exactly

**No transpose.** Element $i$ of $y$ is $\beta y_i$, then the products
$(\alpha x_j)\,a_{ij}$ added in ascending $j$: the plain column-axpy
definition, which the four-column shape reproduces bit for bit
(tested by replay). $\beta = 0$ never reads $y$; $\alpha = 0$ never
reads $A$ or $x$.

**Transposed.** Element $j$ of $y$ is $\beta y_j$ plus $\alpha$ times
the dot product of column $j$ with $x$, and that dot product is the
Level 1 `dot` kernel's fold: four accumulator registers (eight lanes
in f64) summed in a fixed order at the end. That fold is the
contract, replayed bit for bit by the tests against a scalar
reimplementation of the same fold; it is a legitimately different
rounding sequence from the sequential sum, equally valid, identical on
every target. The conjugate-transpose form folds the conjugated
products; conjugation is an exact sign flip, so `zgemv_c(A)` equals
`zgemv_t(conj A)` bitwise (tested).

## How it is computed

**No transpose: four-column fan-in.** Groups of four columns of $A$
stream through one pass over $y$ (`daxpy4in` and its twins in
`src/kernels.rs`), so $y$'s read-modify-write traffic drops four times
against one `axpy` per column; tail columns run the Level 1 `axpy`.
The complex kernels use the two-multiply lane form of the complex
product, bit-exactly the canonical product order of `C64`/`C32`.

**Transposed: one reduction stream per column.** Each column of $A$ is
dotted with $x$ by the `dot` kernel (`zdotu`/`zdotc` for the complex
forms), and $y_j$ is updated with the result.

A fused multiply-add variant measured faster in the campaign and is
deliberately not used (constraint 2 in `DESIGN.md`: relaxed-SIMD fused
multiply-add rounds differently across hardware).

## Performance

n = 2048, percentage of the fastest same-run memory stream, two runner
draws (the Level 2 README has the whole table and its provenance):
gemv f64 57–64%, f32 59–64%, c64 50–56%, c32 60–63%; gemv_t f64
43–52%, f32 66–68%, c64 and c32 38–42% (gemv_c the same). The
no-transpose form is bounded by touching two vectors per group of
columns; the transposed form by the reduction's fold. No comparison
against OpenBLAS or BLIS yet (a recorded lever).

## Tests

`tests/L2/dgemv.rs`, `sgemv.rs`, `zgemv.rs`, `cgemv.rs`: the
no-transpose form replayed bit for bit against the scalar definition;
the transposed forms replayed against the dot kernel's fold (the
replay itself checked against the public `dot` routines at every tail
length) and bounded against the no-transpose call on an explicit
transpose; `zgemv_c == zgemv_t(conj A)` bitwise; `semantics.rs` for
$\alpha = 0$ and $\beta = 0$; `errors.rs` for the argument checks.
